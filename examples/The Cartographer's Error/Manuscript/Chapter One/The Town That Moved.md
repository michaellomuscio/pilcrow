The village of Ardmore had been in the wrong place for eleven years before anyone official noticed, and by then it had stopped being a mistake and started being a habit.

Nell found it the way she found most things, which was by refusing to accept that a line on paper could be wrong. The 1961 survey put Ardmore two miles east of the river. The 1972 survey put it two miles west. Both had been signed, stamped, and filed by men who were very sure of themselves, and neither had thought to walk down and look.

She walked down and looked.

What she found was a church, a petrol station, and forty-one houses arranged along a road that appeared on no map she had ever been given. The road had a name. The name was painted on a sign, and the sign had been repainted recently, which meant somebody cared enough to keep it legible.

---

"You're the map woman," said the man at the petrol station. It was not a question, and he did not seem surprised, which unsettled her more than hostility would have.

"I'm from the Ordnance Survey."

"Same thing." He wiped his hands on a rag that had long since given up being clean. "You'll want Mrs. Aiken. She keeps the record."

Nell had spent nine years correcting other people's errors and had never once been told that the error kept a record of itself.
