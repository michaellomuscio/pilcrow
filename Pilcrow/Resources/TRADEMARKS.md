# Trademark policy

Pilcrow's **code** is open source under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE).
You may use it, study it, modify it, and redistribute it — including modified
versions. That freedom is deliberate and it is not going away.

Pilcrow's **name and identity** are a separate thing, and they are not part of
that grant. Apache 2.0 section 6 already withholds trademark rights; this
document says plainly what that means in practice.

## The reserved marks

- **Pilcrow**, as the name of this application
- **Lomuscio Labs**
- The pilcrow (**¶**) application icon
- The associated visual identity — the purple and near-black brand system

## What you can do without asking

- Run, use, and share **unmodified** builds, including the signed release
- Redistribute the source, unmodified, with this licence intact
- Say truthfully that your project is *based on* or *a fork of* Pilcrow
- Fork the repository on GitHub and keep the name there while you work
- Write about it, review it, teach with it, screenshot it

## What a modified build has to do

If you distribute a build you have changed, you must:

1. **Rename the application.** It cannot be called Pilcrow.
2. **Change the bundle identifier** away from `com.lomusciolabs.*`.
3. **Remove the Lomuscio Labs name**, the ¶ icon, and the brand colours from
   the application and from anything you publish about it.
4. **Not imply endorsement.** "Based on Pilcrow" is fine. "Pilcrow Pro",
   "Pilcrow by …", or anything that reads as an official version is not.

## Why it works this way

Modification is welcome — that is the whole point of publishing the source.
What is not welcome is a modified build going out under this name and this
brand, because someone downloading it has no way to tell whose work they are
actually running, and no way to know whether the thing that just ate their
manuscript was mine or a stranger's.

Rename it and the problem disappears. You keep every freedom the licence
gives you; readers keep the ability to tell the difference.
